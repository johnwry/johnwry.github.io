I ask, then: Did they stumble so as to fall? 

​	Certainly not! 

​	But through their trespass, salvation has come to the Gentiles, 

​		to provoke them to jealousy. 

​		Now if their trespass means riches for the world, 

​		and their loss means riches for the Gentiles, 

​			how much more will their fullness mean! 

Now I am speaking to you Gentiles. Inasmuch as I am an apostle to the Gentiles, I honor my ministry, hoping somehow to provoke my own people to jealousy and save some of them.

​	For if their rejection means reconciliation for the world, what will their 		acceptance mean but life from the dead? 

​	If the first portion of the dough is holy, so is the whole batch. 

​	And if the root is holy, so are the branches. 

​	But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, though a wild olive 		branch, were grafted in among them and came to share with them in the 		olive tree’s nourishing root, do not boast over the branches. 

​	But if you do boast, it is not you who support the root; the root supports you. You will say, then, “Branches were broken off so that I might be grafted in. Granted. 

They were broken off because of unbelief, but you stand by faith. 

Do not be arrogant, but fear. 

​	For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you. Consider, then, God’s kindness and severity: severity toward those who have fallen, but God’s kindness toward you, if you continue in his kindness. Otherwise, you too will be cut off. And they too, if they do not continue in unbelief, will be grafted in, for God is able to graft them in again. For if you were cut from an olive tree that is wild by nature, and contrary to nature were grafted into a cultivated olive tree, how much more will these natural branches be grafted into their own olive tree! For I do not want you to be unaware of this mystery, brothers and sisters, so that you will not be wise in your own estimation: a partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles comes in. And in this way all Israel will be saved, just as it is written: “The Deliverer will come from Zion; he will turn ungodliness away from Jacob. And this is my covenant with them, when I take away their sins.” With regard to the gospel, they are enemies for your sake; but with regard to election, they are beloved for the fathers’ sake. For God’s gifts and calling are irrevocable. For just as you were once disobedient to God but have now received mercy through their disobedience, so they too have now been disobedient, so that through the mercy shown to you they too may now receive mercy. For God has confined all to disobedience so that he might show mercy to all. Oh, the depth of God’s riches and wisdom and knowledge!
How unsearchable are his judgments,
and how untraceable his ways! “For who has known the Lord’s mind?
Or who has become his counselor?” “Or who has given to him first,
so that he must be repaid?” For from him and through him and to him are all things.
To him be the glory forever. Amen.

### 11 THEREFORE I ask:
​    OBS: The speaker uses the first-person singular, “I.”
​    Q: What preceding statement does THEREFORE connect with? 
​	A: (inference): Most immediately, THEREFORE follows the account of Israel’s failure to obtain what it sought and the hardening described in verses 7–10. Paul now asks what their stumbling means. This continues the larger question of whether God has rejected his people in verses 1–2. The connector marks the transition, but does not itself specify one exclusive preceding sentence. Romans 1:1-11

### 11 Did they stumble
​    OBS: The subject of “stumble” is plural in the Greek.

### 11     SO THAT they might fall?

​        OBS: SO THAT connects the falling with the stumbling in the question.
​        OBS: “Fall” is the same Greek verb used for “those who have fallen” in verse 22.

### 11 May it never be!

​    OBS: A negative response follows the question.

### 11 BUT through their trespass salvation [has come] to the Gentiles,

​    OBS: Their trespass is connected with salvation coming to the Gentiles.
​    Q: Who are “they” and “their” in this verse? 
​	A (inference): They are Israelites in the discussion, particularly those whose unbelief and stumbling Paul is describing. Israel is named in verse 7, Paul identifies his own people in verses 13–14, and verse 25 names Israel again. We should retain his distinction between the believing remnant and the hardened remainder rather than assume every Israelite has the same condition.   Romans 11:5–7, 13–14, 25

### 11     TO provoke them to jealousy.

​        OBS: The stated purpose concerns provoking “them” to jealousy.
​        OBS: The verb for provoking jealousy recurs in verse 14.

### 12     BUT IF their trespass [is] riches for the world

​        OBS: IF introduces a condition connecting their trespass with riches for the world.

### 12     AND their loss [is] riches for the Gentiles,

​        OBS: AND joins a second statement to the condition.
​        OBS: “Riches” is repeated, with “Gentiles” corresponding to “world” in the preceding line.

### 12 HOW MUCH MORE their fullness [will mean riches]!

​    OBS: HOW MUCH MORE introduces a comparison with their fullness.
​    OBS: The same Greek noun for “fullness” occurs with “Gentiles” in verse 25.
​    Q: What does “their fullness” describe? 
​	A (inference): Their fullness is the positive counterpart to their trespass and loss. Their acceptance in verse 15 and their being grafted in again in verses 23–24 support reading it as Israel’s restored or full participation in the blessing under discussion. The verse does not specify a numerical total or explain precisely how that fullness is measured.       Romans 11:11–15, 23–26

### 13 BUT I speak to you Gentiles.

​    OBS: The audience is explicitly addressed as Gentiles.
​    OBS: “You” is plural in the Greek here.

### 13     INASMUCH AS, THEN, I am INDEED an apostle to the Gentiles,

​        OBS: The speaker identifies himself as an apostle to Gentiles.

### 13 I honor my ministry,

​    OBS: The first-person singular speaker calls the ministry “my ministry.”

### 14     IF SOMEHOW I might provoke my own people to jealousy

​        OBS: “My own people” renders the Greek expression “my flesh.”
​        Q: How does IF SOMEHOW relate these actions to honoring the ministry?
​		A (inference): The phrase expresses Paul’s hoped-for outcome in honoring his ministry to Gentiles: provoking his own people to jealousy and saving some of them. Verse 11 has already connected Gentile salvation with provoking them to jealousy. This wording gives his aim; it does not say every person provoked will be saved. Romans 11:11, 13–14

### 14     AND save some of them.

​        OBS: AND joins saving to provoking jealousy.
​        OBS: The stated object of “save” is “some of them.”

### 15     FOR IF their rejection [is] reconciliation for the world,

​        OBS: FOR introduces a condition connecting their rejection with reconciliation for the world.

### 15 what [will] their acceptance [be], EXCEPT life from the dead?

​    OBS: The question contrasts their acceptance with their rejection.
​    OBS: “Life from the dead” follows EXCEPT.
​    Q: What does “life from the dead” describe in this comparison?
​	A (open): The phrase describes the extraordinary positive outcome of their acceptance, contrasted with their rejection bringing reconciliation to the world. Whether “life from the dead” identifies bodily resurrection or uses resurrection language for a life-giving restoration is not settled by this sentence. Romans elsewhere uses life-from-death language in more than one setting, so the image alone should not decide the referent. Romans 11:15, 6:13, 8:11

### 16     BUT IF the firstfruits [are] holy,

​        OBS: The first IF statement names firstfruits and calls them holy.
​        Q: What do the firstfruits represent?
​		A (open): The firstfruits are the initial portion whose holiness is connected with the whole batch. Numbers 15 supplies a close dough-offering background. The believing Jewish remnant and the patriarchs are possible historical referents suggested by Romans 11:5 and 11:28 respectively. Paul also uses firstfruits language for the Spirit, a convert, and Christ’s resurrection elsewhere. None of those other uses explicitly identifies the firstfruits in this verse. Leave the historical identification open. Numbers 15:17–21; Romans 11:5, 16, 28, 8:23, 16:5; 1 Corinthians 15:20–23

### 16 the whole batch of dough [is holy] ALSO.

​    OBS: The corresponding statement applies holiness to the whole batch of dough.
​    OBS: “Is holy” is supplied in English from the preceding clause.

### 16     AND IF the root [is] holy,

​        OBS: AND introduces a second IF statement, this time concerning the root.
​        Q: What does the root represent?
​		A (open): The root is described as holy, nourishing, and supportive. Romans 15:12 supplies a substantial comparison by calling the Messiah the root of Jesse; its surrounding verses also join Christ, the fathers’ promises, and mercy to Gentiles. The fathers in Romans 11:28 and the natural branches’ prior relationship in verse 24 also support considering the patriarchs. Romans 11 does not explicitly name the root. These comparisons support candidates, not a settled identification. Romans 11:16–18, 24, 28, 15:8–12

### 16 the branches [are holy] ALSO.

​    OBS: The corresponding statement applies holiness to the branches.
​    OBS: The two illustrations follow the same IF / ALSO pattern.

### 17     BUT IF some of the branches were broken off,

​        OBS: The wording specifies “some” of the branches.
​        OBS: “Were broken off” is passive in the Greek.
​        Q: Who performs the breaking off and the grafting described here?
​		A (inference): God is the strongest contextual identification of the acting agent. The passive verbs in verse 17 do not name the agent there, but verse 21 says God did not spare the natural branches, verse 22 names his severity, and verse 23 expressly names God as able to graft them in again. Their unbelief is the stated reason for being broken off in verse 20. Their responsibility and God’s action should both be retained. Romans 11:17, 20–23

### 17     BUT you were grafted in among them,

​        OBS: The address changes to singular “you.”
​        OBS: The grafting is described as “among them.”

### 17         though being a wild olive,

​            OBS: “Being” is a participle in the Greek.
​            Q: How does “being a wild olive” relate to the grafting statement?
​		A (inference): The participle describes the singular “you” in the grafting statement: you were a wild olive and were grafted in among them. Rendering the relation concessively as “though being” fits the contrast with natural branches and the contrary-to-nature grafting in verse 24. “Though” is an English explanation of that relationship, not a separate Greek conjunction in verse 17. Romans 11:17, 24

### 17     AND came to share in the olive tree’s nourishing root,

​        OBS: The Greek describes “you” as a fellow participant in the root.
​        OBS: The root is mentioned here between its mentions in verses 16 and 18.

### 18 do not boast over the branches.

​    OBS: This is a prohibition addressed to singular “you.”
​    Q: Which branches are referred to in this prohibition?
​	A (inference): The preceding natural branches provide the contextual referent. The imagined reply in verse 19 singles out branches broken off, which makes them especially relevant to the prohibition. The bare expression “the branches” in verse 18 does not explicitly restrict its scope to the broken-off branches alone. Romans 11:17–20

### 18     BUT IF you boast,

​        OBS: The same Greek verb for boasting occurs in the prohibition and this condition.

### 18 you do not support the root,

​    OBS: “You” is the subject and “the root” is the object of “support.”

### 18 BUT the root [supports] you.

​    OBS: BUT reverses the relationship: the root supports “you.”
​    OBS: The verb is understood rather than repeated in the Greek.

### 19 THEREFORE you will say:

​    OBS: A future verb introduces what singular “you” will say.

### 19 “Branches were broken off

​    OBS: “Were broken off” repeats the same Greek verb form used in verse 17.

### 19     SO THAT I might be grafted in.”

​        OBS: The quoted purpose clause contains an explicit first-person singular “I.”
​        Q: What relationship between breaking off and grafting in is asserted in this quotation?
​		A (text): The quoted speaker presents the branches’ breaking off as serving the purpose of his being grafted in. Paul responds “Granted,” then specifies unbelief as the reason they were broken off and faith as the basis of the addressee’s standing. We should preserve both the quoted claim and Paul’s qualification. Verse 17 has said only some branches were broken off and that the Gentile was grafted in among others. Romans 11:17, 19–20

### 20 Granted.

​    OBS: “Granted” renders a Greek adverb, not a finite verb.

### 20 They were broken off because of unbelief,

​    OBS: “They” is plural in the Greek.
​    OBS: Unbelief is connected with being broken off.
​    OBS: “Were broken off” is passive in the Greek.

### 20 BUT you stand by faith.

​    OBS: Faith is connected with standing.
​    OBS: “You” is singular in the Greek.
​    Q: Whom does the singular “you” represent?
​	A (inference): The singular “you” represents the Gentile audience Paul explicitly addresses in verse 13, pictured through the wild olive in verses 17–24. It can address a representative Gentile hearer or reader. The identification of the audience is stronger than any further claim that the warning concerns only a whole Gentile group, only individuals, or both. The pronoun’s number does not alone settle that scope. Romans 11:13, 17–25

​    Q: What does “standing” describe here?
​	A (inference): In this illustration, standing describes the addressee’s existing participation, contrasted with the branches broken off. Verse 17 has described sharing the root’s nourishment; verse 20 explicitly relates standing to faith. Romans 5:2 also connects standing with grace and access through faith. The word “standing” by itself does not define every spiritual consequence of subsequently being cut off. Romans 11:17–22, 5:2

### 20 Do not be arrogant,

​    OBS: This is a prohibition.

### 20 BUT fear.

​    OBS: This is a positive command.

### 21     FOR IF God did not spare the natural branches,

​        OBS: God is explicitly named as the subject in the IF clause.

### 21 neither will he spare you.

​    OBS: The same Greek verb for sparing occurs in both clauses.
​    OBS: The second verb is future in the Greek.

### 22 THEREFORE consider God’s kindness AND severity:

​    OBS: A singular command introduces both kindness and severity.

### 22 on the one hand, toward those who have fallen, [there is] severity;

​    OBS: “Those who have fallen” is plural.
​    OBS: “Fallen” repeats the Greek verb used in the question in verse 11.

### 22 BUT toward you, [there is] God’s kindness,

​    OBS: BUT contrasts kindness toward “you” with severity toward those who have fallen.

### 22     IF you continue in his kindness,

​        OBS: The kindness toward “you” is qualified by IF.
​        OBS: “Kindness” occurs three times in this verse.
​        Q: What does continuing in his kindness involve?
​		A (inference): The immediate context supports remaining in faith and dependence on God’s kindness. Verse 20 contrasts unbelief with faith; verse 23 contrasts continuing in unbelief with being grafted in. Nevertheless, the exact wording here is “continue in his kindness.” Understanding it through the faith/unbelief contrast is a contextual inference, not an identical phrase substituted by Paul. Romans 11:20–23

### 22     SINCE otherwise you ALSO will be cut off.

​        OBS: “You also” is included in the warning.
​        OBS: “Will be cut off” is future and passive in the Greek.
​        Q: How does this warning connect with the preceding IF clause?
​		A (text): The warning states the consequence associated with failing to continue in God’s kindness: “you also will be cut off.” The same singular addressee receives both the condition and the warning. This follows the comparison with God not sparing the natural branches. The sentence states the warning clearly; its precise consequence beyond the tree illustration needs a further argument. Romans 11:21–23

### 23     BUT IF they do not continue in unbelief,

​        OBS: The verb for “continue” repeats the verb in verse 22.
​        OBS: Here continuing concerns unbelief and is negated.

### 23 they ALSO will be grafted in.

​    OBS: “They also” is the plural subject of a future passive verb.
​    OBS: Grafting in is connected with not continuing in unbelief.

### 23 FOR God is able

​    OBS: God is explicitly named as the one who is able.

### 23     to graft them in AGAIN.

​        OBS: The infinitive specifies what God is able to do.
​        OBS: AGAIN accompanies the grafting.

### 24     FOR IF you were cut from an olive tree that is wild by nature

​        OBS: Singular “you” is described as having been cut from a wild olive.
​        OBS: The Greek verb for cutting is the same verb used for cutting off in verse 22.

### 24     AND were grafted, contrary to nature, into a cultivated olive tree,

​        OBS: AND joins the grafting to the cutting in the IF construction.
​        OBS: The wording contrasts “by nature” with “contrary to nature.”

### 24 HOW MUCH MORE will these natural branches be grafted into their own olive tree!

​    OBS: HOW MUCH MORE repeats the comparative expression used in verse 12.
​    OBS: The tree is called “their own olive tree.”
​    Q: What relationship is expressed by “their own”?
​	A (inference): Their own marks the natural branches’ prior relationship to the cultivated olive tree, contrasted with the wild origin of the Gentile addressee. The fathers and Israel’s covenant privileges in Romans 9 and 11 support understanding this as an ancestral and covenant relationship. The text nevertheless conditions their grafting in again on not continuing in unbelief; natural belonging does not erase that stated condition. Romans 11:23–24, 28–29, 9:4–5

### 25 FOR I do not want, brothers and sisters,

​    OBS: The address returns to plural “you” in the Greek.

### 25     you to be unaware of this mystery—

​        OBS: The speaker names “this mystery” as what the readers should not be unaware of.

### 25     (SO THAT you may not be wise in your own estimation)—

​        OBS: The stated purpose is that they may not be wise in their own estimation.

### 25         THAT a partial hardening has happened to Israel

​            OBS: The statement names Israel and includes the qualification “in part.”
​            Q: What is qualified by “partial”?
​		A (inference): The strongest contextual reading is that the hardening affects part of Israel rather than Israel without remainder. Verses 5–7 distinguish the remnant from the hardened rest. The following UNTIL clause separately introduces a temporal reference. This supports distinguishing the extent of the hardening from the question of its duration. Romans 11:5–7, 25

### 25             UNTIL the fullness of the Gentiles comes in,

​                OBS: UNTIL connects the hardening with the coming in of the Gentiles’ fullness.
​                OBS: “Fullness” repeats the Greek noun used in verse 12.
​                Q: What does “the fullness of the Gentiles” describe?
​			A (inference): The phrase concerns Gentile inclusion reaching its fullness or completion. The context has discussed salvation and mercy reaching Gentiles and their participation through faith. The sentence does not enumerate a fixed total or define the precise historical extent of that fullness. “The full number” is a possible explanatory rendering, but our present text properly retains “fullness” while the exact sense remains under examination. Romans 11:11–13, 17–25, 30

### 26         AND IN THIS WAY all Israel will be saved,

​            OBS: The statement begins AND IN THIS WAY.
​            OBS: “All Israel” is the subject of “will be saved,” a future passive verb.
​            Q: Does this statement continue the content of “this mystery”?
​		A (inference): It is natural to read both the hardening statement and the coordinated salvation statement as the mystery’s content: THAT a hardening has happened … AND IN THIS WAY all Israel will be saved. That is the provisional attachment shown in our outline. The connector AND does not by itself mark where the earlier THAT-clause ends, so treating verse 26 as a new main assertion remains an alternative grammatical analysis. Romans 11:25–26

​            Q: Whom does “all Israel” identify?
​		A (inference): Israel, distinguished from Gentiles in verse 25 and then called Jacob in the quotation, is the strongest immediate contextual referent. The fathers in verse 28 continue that connection. The exact extent of “all” still needs examination: Romans 9:6–8 and 9:27 prevent us from simply equating physical descent with receipt of every saving promise. This first pass does not settle the membership or timing represented by “all Israel.” Romans 11:25–29, 9:6–8, 27

### 26             JUST AS it is written:

​                OBS: JUST AS introduces a quotation as written text.
​                Q: How does the quotation relate to the statement about Israel’s salvation?
​			A (inference): The quotation supports the salvation statement with written promises of deliverance, the turning away of ungodliness from Jacob, and God taking away their sins in connection with his covenant. Isaiah 59:20–21 and 27:9 provide close textual connections; Jeremiah 31:31–34 supplies a parallel covenant-and-forgiveness promise. Romans 11:27 itself says “covenant” without adding “new.” The quotations should be read in their wording in Romans, which is not identical to the Hebrew in every detail. Romans 11:26–27; Isaiah 59:20–21, 27:9; Jeremiah 31:31–34

### 26                 “The Deliverer will come from Zion;

​                    OBS: The Deliverer is named as the subject of the future verb “will come.”

### 26                 he will turn ungodliness away from Jacob.

​                    OBS: Ungodliness is what will be turned away from Jacob.

### 27                 AND this [is] my covenant with them,

​                    OBS: The quotation uses first-person “my” and plural “them.”
​                    OBS: The English “is” supplies a verb not expressed in the Greek.
​                    Q: Who is speaking within the quotation, and whom does “them” identify?
​			A (inference): God speaks within the covenant quotation: Isaiah 59:21 explicitly identifies the covenant speaker as Yahweh. “Them” most naturally refers to Jacob/Israel, named immediately before it and throughout verses 25–28. Paul is quoting God’s covenant commitment, rather than presenting the first-person words as his own covenant. Romans 11:25–28; Isaiah 59:20–21

### 27                     WHEN I take away their sins.”

​                        OBS: WHEN connects taking away sins with the covenant statement.
​                        OBS: “I” is the subject of taking away, and “their sins” is the object.

### 28 With regard to the gospel, [they are] enemies for your sake;

​    OBS: The description “enemies” is qualified by “with regard to the gospel.”
​    OBS: “Your” is plural in the Greek.

### 28 BUT with regard to election, [they are] beloved for the fathers’ sake.

​    OBS: The corresponding description “beloved” is qualified by “with regard to election.”
​    OBS: “For your sake” is paired with “for the fathers’ sake.”
​    Q: Who are described by these two statements?
​	A (inference): The same Israelites under discussion are described from two stated perspectives: enemies with regard to the gospel, yet beloved with regard to election because of the fathers. The contrast with “you” and “these” in verses 30–31 supports the Israel/Gentile distinction. The sentence preserves both descriptions; it does not tell us to discard one of them. Romans 11:25–31

### 29 FOR God’s gifts AND calling [are] irrevocable.

​    OBS: FOR introduces an explanation following verse 28.
​    OBS: “Irrevocable” describes the gifts and calling of God.
​    Q: Which gifts and calling are in view?
​	A (inference): Most immediately, the gifts and calling concern God’s relationship to Israel and explain the preceding description of being beloved because of the fathers. Romans 9:4–5 has already listed Israel’s privileges, covenants, promises, and fathers. That is a strong contextual connection, but verse 29 does not itemize the gifts or separately define the calling. A more detailed identification should remain provisional. Romans 11:28–29, 9:4–5

### 30     FOR JUST AS you once disobeyed God,

​        OBS: JUST AS begins a comparison completed by SO in verse 31.
​        OBS: “You” is plural in the Greek.

### 30     BUT now have received mercy through their disobedience,

**OBS: Negative circumstances repeatedly appear alongside positive outcomes.**

- Trespass → salvation to Gentiles.
- Trespass and loss → riches for the world and Gentiles.
- Rejection → reconciliation for the world.
- Disobedience → mercy.

**OBS: The discussion repeatedly looks beyond the present negative condition.**
Their **fullness**, **acceptance**, being grafted in **again**, and Israel’s future salvation recur across the passage. Romans 11:12, 15, 23–27

**OBS: The two illustrations in verse 16 share the same grammatical pattern.**
**IF** the first portion is holy, the larger whole is holy **ALSO**. The dough illustration ends there; the root-and-branches illustration continues through verse 24.

**OBS: The extended tree illustration contains both warnings and statements about grafting in again.**
The Gentile addressee receives prohibitions and commands: do not boast, do not be arrogant, fear, and consider. Alongside these are statements that the broken branches can be grafted in again. Romans 11:18–24

**OBS: Faith and unbelief connect the two sides of the tree illustration.**
Unbelief accompanies being broken off; faith accompanies standing. Continuing in kindness is followed by a warning, while not continuing in unbelief is followed by the promise of grafting in. Romans 11:20–23

**OBS: The explanation of the mystery is surrounded by language addressing the readers’ attitude.**
Before it, Paul prohibits boasting and arrogance. When introducing it, he states his purpose: that the readers may not be wise in their own estimation. Romans 11:18, 20, 25

**OBS: The passage repeatedly supplies reasons, conditions, purposes, and comparisons.**
**FOR**, **IF**, **SO THAT**, **JUST AS / SO**, and **HOW MUCH MORE** connect statements throughout the discussion. The passage develops through these connections rather than presenting isolated assertions.

**OBS: Mercy becomes concentrated immediately before the praise.**
Mercy vocabulary recurs throughout verses 30–32. Verse 32 gathers the preceding comparison into one statement about disobedience and mercy; verse 33 then begins the exclamation concerning God’s riches, wisdom, and knowledge.

**OBS: The ending repeatedly directs attention to God.**
His judgments and ways, the Lord’s mind, his counselor, giving to him, and all things being **FROM him**, **THROUGH him**, and **TO him** lead into “To him [be] the glory.” Romans 11:33–36

**The strongest overall purpose is to explain how Israel’s unbelief and Gentile salvation fit with God’s word not having failed.** That is a conclusion about the argument, supported by Paul’s explicit questions and answers.

The flow becomes visible when we follow those questions:

1. **Israel has the promises—yet Paul grieves over Israel.**
   Paul begins with sorrow for his own people, then names their privileges: adoption, glory, covenants, law, worship, promises, fathers, and the Messiah’s ancestry. This places Israel’s present condition alongside what God has given them. Romans 9:1–5
2. **Has God’s word failed? No.**
   Paul explicitly denies this. He then distinguishes physical descent from the children of promise, discusses God’s choosing and mercy, and brings both Gentile calling and Israel’s remnant into the discussion. Romans 9:6–29
3. **Why, then, did Israel stumble?**
   Paul gives an explicit answer: they pursued righteousness without faith, as though through works. Chapter 10 develops this discussion through righteousness, believing, hearing, and disobedience. His prayer remains that they would be saved. Romans 9:30–33, 10:1–21
4. **Does their disobedience mean God rejected his people? No.**
   Paul answers with himself and the existence of a remnant. Israel’s unbelief is not the condition of every Israelite. Romans 11:1–10
5. **Does their stumbling end the discussion? No.**
   Salvation has reached Gentiles, with the stated purpose of provoking Israel to jealousy. Paul speaks of their fullness, acceptance, and being grafted in again. The illustration warns Gentiles against boasting while keeping Israel’s grafting in before them. Romans 11:11–24
6. **What does Paul place at the conclusion?**
   Partial hardening, Gentile fullness, Israel’s salvation, God’s covenant, and the irrevocability of his gifts and calling. He then brings both groups together in the discussion of disobedience and mercy. Romans 11:25–32
7. **The explanation leads into praise.**
   Paul concludes by praising God’s wisdom, knowledge, judgments, and ways. Romans 11:33–36

There is also an **explicit purpose directed at the readers**: Paul explains the mystery **SO THAT they may not be wise in their own estimation**. His explanation should produce humility toward Israel and recognition of God’s mercy. Romans 11:25, 30–32

This gives us a useful connection with chapter 8, although the connection is an inference: after affirming confidence in God’s saving purpose, Paul addresses Israel’s situation—the apparent difficulty that could raise questions about confidence in God’s promises. Romans 8:28–39, 9:1–6

**11**

> THEREFORE I ask: Did they stumble SO THAT they might fall? May it never be! BUT through their trespass salvation [has come] to the Gentiles, TO provoke them to jealousy.

Paul rejects the proposed purpose of their stumbling. Their trespass brought salvation to Gentiles, with the purpose of provoking them to jealousy.

**12**

> BUT IF their trespass [is] riches for the world AND their loss [is] riches for the Gentiles, HOW MUCH MORE their fullness [will mean riches]!

Paul compares the riches associated with their trespass and loss with the greater outcome associated with their fullness.

**13**

> BUT I speak to you Gentiles. INASMUCH AS, THEN, I am INDEED an apostle to the Gentiles, I honor my ministry,

Paul directly addresses Gentiles and explains his regard for his ministry to them.

**14**

> IF SOMEHOW I might provoke my own people to jealousy AND save some of them.

His ministry includes the hope of provoking his own people to jealousy and saving some of them.

**15**

> FOR IF their rejection [is] reconciliation for the world, what [will] their acceptance [be], EXCEPT life from the dead?

Paul contrasts their rejection with their acceptance, describing the latter in terms of life from the dead.

**16**

> BUT IF the firstfruits [are] holy, the whole batch of dough [is holy] ALSO. AND IF the root [is] holy, the branches [are holy] ALSO.

Two illustrations connect holiness: firstfruits with dough, and root with branches. Neither illustration explicitly identifies what its parts represent.

**17**

> BUT IF some of the branches were broken off, BUT you were grafted in among them, though being a wild olive, AND came to share in the olive tree’s nourishing root,

Some branches were broken off. The person addressed was grafted in among others and became a fellow participant in the root’s nourishment.

**18**

> Do not boast over the branches. BUT IF you boast, you do not support the root, BUT the root [supports] you.

Paul prohibits boasting. The recipient depends on the root for support.

**19**

> THEREFORE you will say: “Branches were broken off SO THAT I might be grafted in.”

Paul introduces a reply presenting the branches’ removal as serving the purpose of the speaker’s grafting in.

**20**

> Granted. They were broken off because of unbelief, BUT you stand by faith. Do not be arrogant, BUT fear.

Paul specifies the distinction: unbelief accompanied their removal; faith accompanies the recipient’s standing. He commands fear rather than arrogance.

**21**

> FOR IF God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you.

God’s treatment of the natural branches supplies the reason for the warning to the recipient.

**22**

> THEREFORE consider God’s kindness AND severity: on the one hand, toward those who have fallen, [there is] severity; BUT toward you, [there is] God’s kindness, IF you continue in his kindness, SINCE otherwise you ALSO will be cut off.

Both kindness and severity must be considered. Continuing in God’s kindness is stated as a condition; otherwise, the recipient also will be cut off.

**23**

> BUT IF they do not continue in unbelief, they ALSO will be grafted in. FOR God is able to graft them in AGAIN.

Their grafting in again is conditional on not continuing in unbelief. God is able to accomplish it.

**24**

> FOR IF you were cut from an olive tree that is wild by nature AND were grafted, contrary to nature, into a cultivated olive tree, HOW MUCH MORE will these natural branches be grafted into their own olive tree!

Paul reasons from the contrary-to-nature grafting to how much more the natural branches will be grafted into their own tree.

**25**

> FOR I do not want, brothers and sisters, you to be unaware of this mystery—SO THAT you may not be wise in your own estimation—THAT a partial hardening has happened to Israel UNTIL the fullness of the Gentiles comes in,

Paul explains the mystery to prevent self-conceit. Israel’s hardening is qualified as partial and connected with an UNTIL statement.

**26**

> AND IN THIS WAY all Israel will be saved, JUST AS it is written: “The Deliverer will come from Zion; he will turn ungodliness away from Jacob.

Paul states Israel’s salvation and supports it with Scripture concerning the Deliverer and the removal of ungodliness from Jacob.

**27**

The covenant statement connects the covenant with the taking away of their sins.

**28**

> With regard to the gospel, [they are] enemies for your sake; BUT with regard to election, [they are] beloved for the fathers’ sake.

The same people receive two descriptions, each qualified by a different consideration: the gospel and election.

**29**

> FOR God’s gifts AND calling [are] irrevocable.

The irrevocability of God’s gifts and calling supplies the reason supporting the preceding statement.

**30**

> FOR JUST AS you once disobeyed God, BUT now have received mercy through their disobedience,

Paul begins a comparison: the readers once disobeyed but now have received mercy through the others’ disobedience.

**31**

> SO these ALSO have now disobeyed, SO THAT they too may now receive mercy through the mercy [shown] to you.

The comparison turns to the others. Their present disobedience is followed by the stated purpose that they too receive mercy.

Both statements concern “all.” Showing mercy is the stated purpose of God’s confinement to disobedience.

Paul praises God’s riches, wisdom, and knowledge, and describes his judgments and ways as beyond searching out.

**What the text establishes**

1. **God’s word has not failed, and he has not rejected his people.** A remnant exists in Paul’s own time. Romans 9:6, 11:1–5
2. **Salvation has reached Gentiles through Israel’s trespass.** Provoking Israel to jealousy is a stated purpose, and Paul hopes to save some of his own people through his ministry. Romans 11:11–14
3. **Some branches were broken off; others remained.** The Gentile recipient was grafted in among them and became a fellow participant in the root’s nourishment. Romans 11:17
4. **Unbelief explains their removal; faith explains the recipient’s standing.** Their natural relationship to the tree did not prevent removal through unbelief. Romans 11:20–21
5. **The Gentile recipient receives an explicit warning.** God’s kindness toward the recipient is qualified by continuing in it; otherwise, the recipient also will be cut off. Romans 11:22
6. **Being broken off does not make grafting in again impossible.** Those who do not continue in unbelief will be grafted in again. God is able to accomplish this. Romans 11:23–24
7. **Israel’s hardening is partial and connected with an UNTIL statement.** Paul then declares that all Israel will be saved and supports this with the covenant quotation concerning the removal of sins. Romans 11:25–27
8. **God’s gifts and calling are irrevocable.** The people described as enemies regarding the gospel are also described as beloved regarding election because of the fathers. Romans 11:28–29
9. Israel’s unbelief has real consequences. God nevertheless remains faithful to his promises, and the broken branches will be grafted in again if they do not continue in unbelief.** Romans 11:20–29
10. **Both groups appear in the account of disobedience and mercy.** God’s stated purpose in verse 32 is to show mercy to all. Romans 11:30–32
11. **The required response includes humility, fear, and recognition of God’s kindness and severity.** Paul opposes boasting, arrogance, and self-conceit, and concludes with praise of God. Romans 11:18–22, 25, 33–36

The illustration gives us enough information to follow its argument without identifying every element. The root nourishes and supports the recipient; the recipient depends on it. Paul supplies the reasons for removal and standing, the condition concerning continued kindness, and the prospect of grafting in again. Romans 11:17–24

**Inferences**

- **11–15:** “They” refers to the Israelites whose stumbling and unbelief Paul has been discussing. Their fullness and acceptance point toward a positive outcome beyond their present condition.
- **17–24:** The singular “you” represents the Gentile audience addressed in verse 13. The singular form alone does not settle whether every aspect concerns individuals, Gentiles collectively, or both.
- **17:** “Though being a wild olive” expresses an inferred contrast. The Greek contains a participle without a separate word meaning “though.”
- **22–23:** Continuing in God’s kindness is closely connected with the surrounding contrast between faith and unbelief.
- **25–27:** Our outline reads Israel’s salvation as part of the mystery’s content. Israel/Jacob is the immediate contextual referent for “them” in the covenant quotation.
- **28–29:** God’s gifts and calling concern his relationship to Israel, particularly in connection with the fathers.
- **31:** Our translation connects “through the mercy [shown] to you” with their receiving mercy. This attachment remains provisional.
- **32:** The preceding comparison indicates that Jews and Gentiles are included in each occurrence of “all.” Whether this entails the eventual salvation of every individual requires further argument; the word alone does not settle that question.
- **34–36:** The questions invite the conclusion that nobody supplies God with counsel or places him under an obligation to repay.
- **The section overall:** God can fulfill his purposes for Israel while individual Israelites miss out through unbelief. Likewise, mercy reaching Gentiles does not mean every Gentile automatically participates. The discussion presents Israel’s unbelief and Gentile inclusion alongside God’s continuing faithfulness to his word and covenant commitment. Romans 9:6, 11:20–32

**Questions that remain open**

Paul distinguishes physical descendants from children of promise. That distinction addresses the assumption that descent guarantees participation; it does not by itself answer what happens to someone already justified through faith. Romans 9:6–8

Romans 8 also gives explicit assurance: God justifies, Christ intercedes, and nothing in creation can separate the people Paul describes from God’s love in Christ. The same discussion connects justification with future glory. Romans 8:28–39

We have not yet established precisely how participation in the olive tree relates to the justified people described in Romans 8. The warning must retain its force, and the assurance must retain its breadth. The consequences of unbelief are clear; the full meaning of being cut off still requires examination.

Other open questions include the identities represented by the firstfruits and root, the precise meaning of life from the dead, and the extent of “all Israel.” Identifying the root may deepen our understanding, but it is not necessary to recognize dependence, the significance of faith and unbelief, or Paul’s correction of Gentile arrogance.

**Reflection**

There is so much human failure in this account, yet God brings the story toward mercy, fulfilled promises, and his own glory. We’re left marveling at what God accomplishes with people like us.
