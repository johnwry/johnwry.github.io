# It Is Settled: original manuscript audit and proposed revision sequence

Prepared for John Wry, October 7, 2026.

## Scope and method

Read the supplied file **it is settled(2).md** in full: approximately 19,463 whitespace-delimited words, including front matter, headings, references, and questions. This is the uploaded original, not a presumed copy of the later rewrite. No manuscript changes were made.

Compared its principal claims and sequence with the biblical text of Romans 1–16 in the World English Bible Classic. Isaiah 53 was also checked for the opening epigraph. Websites supplied biblical text only; no theological commentary was consulted. The audit identifies major textual and structural issues; it is not a completed verification of every quotation or an exhaustive resolution of every interpretive question.

## Overall finding

The original expresses John's intended distinction more clearly than the confidence-centered direction the later assistance took. It repeatedly insists that standing before God has an objective basis in God's action. That direction should be recovered.

Nevertheless, the original often announces the conclusion instead of establishing it from Paul's reasoning. It alternates among three subjects: what God has done; a theory of displaced reference points; and the reader's inner experience. Revision must distinguish them. God's work must receive the sustained explanation that abstract language and emotional applications currently receive.

This is a finding about the manuscript's demonstration, not a declaration that its central thesis has been refuted. The answer is to develop the biblical argument and examine apparent objections, not dilute the book into cautious encouragement.

## What should survive

- The opening conviction that God's truth precedes human interpretation. Introduce this through Romans' gospel of God, promised in Scripture, concerning his Son (1:1–4), rather than a long theory of how all human thought functions.
- The distinction between peace with God and feeling peaceful. Romans 5:1 and 5:9–11 supply the relational setting.
- Movement 5's observation: “The position does not appear when it is discovered, and it does not disappear when it is overlooked.” Preserve the distinction while showing its scriptural grounds.
- The emphasis that Christ's work cannot be supplemented by self-justification. Develop Romans 3:21–28 and 4:1–8 rather than repeating sufficiency language.
- John's true missionary testimony and the question that exposed reliance on his service. Preserve it as testimony, not proof of Paul's meaning or a required emotional journey for every reader.
- The instinct to connect God's faithfulness with his promises. Rebuild the Romans 9–11 treatment from Paul's actual argument rather than a general historical analogy.

## The lesson from the previous mistake

The original already contains language such as “Here the relief begins,” “What it eliminates is the constant fear,” and “Force replaces tension. Peace replaces pressure.” The previous editorial approach amplified this strand instead of consistently distinguishing it from what God establishes.

Do not repair this by mechanically deleting human pronouns. Paul speaks about faith, obedience, suffering, hope, and joy. Repair it by asking what a paragraph establishes and why it belongs at that point in Romans. The reader's emotional response must not become the explanation of God's action, its evidence, or the assumed result of reading correctly.

One short distinction between standing and its recognition can prevent confusion. Repeating it in every chapter would make reader awareness central again.

## Where the strongest demonstration is missing

### Romans 3:21–26: how God justifies righteously

The section “What He Did” repeatedly says Christ supplied the solution, but provides little explanation of 3:25–26. The manuscript needs to show why Paul speaks of Christ's blood, an atoning sacrifice, God's forbearance, and the demonstration of God's righteousness. This is essential to the author's insistence that God has not ignored sin.

The present account treats redemption mainly as another word for a successful solution. It needs an explanation rooted in the passage. The result should make clear why God's justifying action is righteous, not merely decisive.

### Romans 4: what God credits and why the promise is sure

The chapter turns early to peace and reconciliation from Romans 5. This displaces David's testimony about forgiveness and non-imputation (4:6–8), the circumcision argument (4:9–12), the promise and grace argument (4:13–17), and Jesus' death and resurrection (4:23–25).

These are substantial contributions, not optional details. God's ability to perform what he promises belongs at the center of the Abraham discussion. The statement that Paul does not present Abraham's strong faith as significant goes too far: 4:19–22 expressly discusses his faith. Explain its relation to God's promise without turning faith into merit or deleting Paul's description.

### Romans 5:9–10: Paul's argument from accomplished reconciliation to future salvation

Movement 5 spends much of its space repeating what “standing” means. The chapter should instead trace Paul's reasoning: Christ died for the ungodly; God demonstrated love toward sinners; those now justified by Christ's blood will be saved through him from wrath; those reconciled through his death will be saved by his life.

This is stronger support than the general maxim “what God establishes, he sustains.” Explain why Paul's conclusion follows from the particular action and relationship he describes.

### Romans 8:30–34: justification, glorification, and Christ's intercession

The existing chapter moves rapidly from no condemnation to life and peace, suffering, and inseparable love. It scarcely develops the passage connecting God's purpose, calling, justification, and glorification, or the questions concerning accusation and condemnation.

The explanation must include Christ's death, resurrection, position at God's right hand, and intercession. The manuscript's appeal to a completed work should not obscure the living Christ's present action. Likewise, established standing should not be confused with the claim that bodily redemption has already occurred: 8:23 places that redemption ahead.

### Romans 14–15: God's acceptance in the life of the church

These chapters are absent from the original, yet 14:3–4 explicitly connects God's acceptance and power to make someone stand with the prohibition on despising or judging a fellow servant. Romans 15:7 grounds mutual welcome in Christ's welcome, to God's glory. These passages belong to the book's subject, alongside 14:10–12's accountability.

## Claims requiring correction or explicit demonstration

1. **“Suppressed ... Deleted.”** Romans 1:18–21 and 1:32 describe suppression alongside knowledge. “Deleted” is not an adequate replacement.
2. **Romans 8:10 used to establish the unconverted person's spiritual death.** Its immediate setting says Christ is in the people described and the Spirit gives life to mortal bodies. The excerpt does not establish the manuscript's claim in that setting.
3. **“The person stands before God's truth without the ability to respond adequately.”** This is too broad for the author's intended limitation. Specify inability to justify oneself by works; Romans 3:22–28 also explicitly speaks of believing.
4. **“You no longer make an effort.”** Romans 4:5 addresses works and justification. It does not cancel Romans 6:12–13's commands, Romans 12:11's diligence, or 15:30's appeal to strive in prayer.
5. **“Behavior flows from identity. Never the other way around.”** Romans 6 connects obedience, slavery, righteousness, and sanctification. Its clauses must be explained before replacing them with an unrestricted maxim.
6. **“Paul does not use fear to motivate.”** Romans 11:20 explicitly tells the addressed Gentile not to be conceited but to fear. Romans 13:4 also appeals to fear of punishment. Distinguish these contexts rather than claiming Paul never makes such appeals.
7. **God is no longer evaluating.** Romans 14:10–12 requires an account before God. Explain the relation between acceptance and accountability; do not erase either.
8. **“Human inconsistency affects the journey, but it does not change the destination.”** As a summary of Romans 9–11, this bypasses 9:6–8, 10:1–4, and 11:17–23. It has not been established by the historical discussion supplied.
9. **Reader discomfort proves a hidden refuge.** “It means that the shelter was deeper than you thought” diagnoses the reader without evidence. Discomfort establishes neither the argument's truth nor its cause.
10. **Understanding automatically produces freedom from fear or less sin.** These experiential generalizations are not demonstrated by the cited texts. Explain the Spirit's action and Paul's commands instead.

These corrections do not require changing the book into a defense of a theological school. Each follows a specific problem in the relationship between the manuscript and the passage cited.

## Questions that must be resolved before strong concluding claims

- How does 2:6–16's judgment according to works relate to 3:19–28? Merely labeling the earlier statements an impossible hypothetical does not explain the text.
- What do obedience, death, sanctification, and eternal life mean in 6:15–23's argument?
- How do 8:9–17's descriptions and conditions relate to 8:30–39? Explain their audiences and connections.
- What is the olive tree in 11:17–24, who is addressed, and what does being cut off signify? Demonstrate the answer before applying the passage to perseverance or to national privilege alone.
- How does God's welcome coexist with accountability and warnings against destroying a brother or God's work in 14:3–23?
- What does “settled” include, and what future acts does Romans still promise? Present justification, Christ's ongoing intercession, and future bodily redemption must not be flattened into a single claim that nothing remains to happen.

The audit has identified these obligations; it does not pretend to have completed their exegesis. In particular, the claim that deliberate repudiation of faith cannot affect standing is not demonstrated merely by showing that feelings do not establish standing. That extension requires its own argument.

## Quotation and language work

Convert quotations individually to WEB, recording reference, wording, source, and verification status. Do not swap the copyright notice while retaining other wording.

WEB Classic differs in more than reading level. Romans 8:1 includes the walking clause; its text must be quoted honestly and the relation to verse 4 examined. Its presentation places the concluding doxology at 14:24–26, with a reference at 16:25. Account for the actual edition when giving references. These are reasons for precise verification, not reasons to evade a difficult wording.

Isaiah 53:11 also requires contextual care: the suffering servant is the immediate subject of seeing and being satisfied. Do not make capitalization in the old epigraph sufficient proof that the Father is the subject. If God's satisfaction is a major formulation, show the argument from the surrounding passage and Romans 3 rather than leaving it to an ambiguous pronoun.

Repair errors such as “lack of nuance,” “What a queen,” “So why Peco?”, pronoun shifts in the testimony, and “it is not a debt to God” followed by an outstanding debt to God. Resolve meaning before stylistic smoothing. Remove empty headings and broken sentence boundaries.

Replace questions that presuppose unproven conclusions. For instance, replace “Why does collapse begin with behavior?” with an observation question about what happens in 1:21–28. Replace emotional self-diagnosis with questions about God's action, Paul's reasons, and the response actually commanded in the passage.

## Proposed next steps

1. **Build the argument before polishing pages.** Prepare a working claim map: claim, passage, explicit statement, interpretive steps, apparent objection, and result. Begin with Romans 3:21–26; 4:1–8, 13–25; 5:1–11; and 8:1–39.
2. **Resolve the major textual tensions.** Examine the passages listed above in their complete arguments. Keep unresolved issues visible; do not insert temporary slogans into the manuscript.
3. **Rebalance the book.** Condense the Prelude and repeated diagnoses. Give Christ's work, Romans 7–8, and Romans 9–11 room for real explanation. Add Romans 12–16.
4. **Draft in the letter's order, one section at a time.** Each section contributes something distinct. Verify quotations while drafting. Preserve the original as source and record adopted revisions separately.
5. **Review the whole argument before the final prose pass.** Ask whether the book demonstrates what God establishes, who is included, why it remains, what Paul commands, and how apparent objections are answered. Then refine transitions, questions, repetition, and wording.

Recommended immediate next deliverable: a close reading and claim map of **Romans 3:21–26**. This establishes the central explanation of God's righteous action before another rewrite of the Prelude begins.
