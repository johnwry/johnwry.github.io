# THE CARNAL CHURCH — Project Instructions

## Purpose and authority

This is John Wry's novel in The Ordinary Somebodies series. Four longtime friends — Adrián, Elías, Marcos and Matías — meet in a café in Windsor, Ontario, and investigate what makes a church carnal. Their discoveries become personal. Enlighten through the story; do not preach at the reader.

These instructions govern all work on the manuscript. Read writing-agent.md for writing or revision and editorial-agent.md for independent editorial review. The author's latest explicit direction takes precedence. Treat the approved manuscript and author-supplied theological sources as the authority on continuity and terminology. When drafts conflict, identify the conflict rather than silently choosing or inventing canon.

## Shared theological framework

- Christ Himself is the believer's life and source of spiritual fruit. Genuine spiritual life is not a product of human personality, effort, religious discipline, doctrinal precision or organisational ability.
- Distinguish the believer's position in Christ from the way the believer actually walks. Use **by the flesh** for the means of operation; **in the flesh** is positional terminology, not an interchangeable description of a believer's conduct.
- Carnality is not confined to immoral behaviour or visibly troubled congregations. Seemingly admirable church activities can also be carried out by the flesh. Do not equate outward success with spiritual health or outward failure with the entire diagnosis.
- A believer has no independent spiritual strength. Genuine commendable works arise through faith and God's work, not the believer's inherent ability. Do not describe divine commendations as evidence of autonomous human strengths.
- Treat Corinth, Galatia, Laodicea and Ephesus according to their actual biblical contexts and the established manuscript argument. Do not flatten their distinctive conditions into one stereotype. The apparently preferable alternatives to Corinth ultimately expose the same issue of carnality.
- In Ephesus, take Christ's commendations seriously and take the fall from first love seriously. Do not assume that abandoning first love is compatible with continuing genuine spiritual work unchanged, or claim the commendations were necessarily flesh-produced. Do not settle ambiguous chronology or motivation without textual support.
- Preserve the distinction between the cross, the believer's position in Christ, and walking in dependence on Him. Grace is not a technique for self-improvement or a licence to sin. Do not substitute sin management or religious performance for the life of Christ.
- Check Scripture references and wording against the text and author-approved translations. Distinguish quotation, inference and interpretation. Do not fabricate citations or impose an outside theological system to fill gaps.

## Story and character continuity

- Maintain the four men's established personalities, relationships, histories and distinct voices. Consult the surrounding manuscript before changing them.
- Marcos and Anna's relationship is incidental to the main thread; references are overheard in passing rather than developed into a competing storyline.
- Spiritual growth is gradual. Even near the conclusion, the men have only begun to discover what it means to live by the power of Christ. They remain ordinary, imperfect human beings, not spiritual giants.
- Show increasingly evident Christlike character as something beyond mere temperament or natural personality improvement. Do not portray fake spirituality, instant transformation or a manufactured spiritual atmosphere.
- Preserve interpersonal tension, unresolved pressures, disagreement and humour. Spiritual growth does not eliminate tension; it may even intensify the pressures they face.
- Keep the cafe and other settings physically present. The reader should feel like a fifth person at the table.

## Shared literary standards

- Write naturally, with human warmth, humour and uneven conversational rhythms. Avoid sterile, mechanical, formulaic or sermon-like prose.
- Keep a speaker's dialogue, speech tags and related actions together in the same paragraph when appropriate. New speaker, new paragraph. Break paragraphs for real changes of focus or narrative purpose.
- Prefer short and medium paragraphs with sufficient breathing room for dyslexic readers. Avoid both excessive fragmentation and dense blocks. Page-count reduction is a benefit, not a reason to sacrifice readability.
- Use occasional organic environmental sounds and natural interruptions during long discussions. Avoid predictable cadence and repetitions.
- Repetition belongs primarily to intentional, character-specific speaking habits, not the narrator's habitual phrasing. Avoid recycled gestures, adverbs, sentence templates and artificial emotional cues. Delete needless beats rather than rotating synonyms.
- Avoid using "but" as a habitual argumentative pivot that dismisses or rolls over a valid preceding observation. Preserve the force of both ideas; retain it where genuinely natural and needed.
- Maintain the manuscript's established spelling, names, accent marks and voice. Do not standardise away meaningful character speech.

## Manuscript stewardship

Read surrounding chapters and established project materials before substantive work. Preserve approved meaning and content during a style-only edit. Flag larger structural or theological changes separately when they exceed the assigned task.

The supplied working manuscript may contain alternate drafts, repeated chapter numbers or incomplete numbering. Verify the intended chapter/version before revising or assembling a definitive manuscript; do not silently delete competing drafts.

Follow the requested scope, preserve original files or revision history, distinguish proposed edits from accepted text, and report unresolved questions honestly. Do not invent author decisions or claim a review occurred when it did not.
