# The Carnal Church — Alternate chapters from v2

These passages come from the working manuscript before the final assembly and WEB verification. The earlier chapter numbers and wording are preserved for reference.

# Chapter ??

## The Correct Church

The conversation slowed again. Three very different pictures of church life were now sitting on the table. A lively church. A serious church. And a letter from Paul describing a divided church full of gifts.

Adrián finally looked at Elías. “You’ve been very quiet.”

Elías smiled faintly. “I’ve been listening.”

“That’s suspicious.”

Marcos laughed. “Why?”

“Because when Elías gets quiet it usually means he’s thinking about a passage somewhere.”

Elías didn’t deny it. “Actually,” he said, “there is one.”

Adrián leaned forward. “Ha ha, of course there is.”

“Where?”

“Revelation.”

Matías looked up. “The letters to the churches?”

“Yes.”

Marcos said, “That could be helpful.”

Elías opened the Bible again and turned a few pages. “This one has always interested me.”

“Which one?” Adrián asked.

“The message to the church at Ephesus.”

Matías leaned closer. “That was a strong church.”

“Yes,” Elías said.

“Very strong.”

Adrián smiled. “Well that’s refreshing.”

“Why?”

“Because so far every church we’ve looked at has been a mess.”

Elías began reading. “I know your works, and your toil and perseverance…”

Matías said, “That’s good.”

“Yes,” Elías said.

He continued. “and that you can’t tolerate evil men…”

Adrián leaned forward. “That’s also good.”

“Yes.”

Elías kept reading. “and have tested those who call themselves apostles, and they are not, and found them false.”

Marcos said, “They tested doctrine.”

“Yes.”

Elías read again. “You have perseverance and have endured for my name’s sake, and have not grown weary.”

Adrián sat back. “Now that sounds like a solid church.”

Matías said, “Yes.”

“They were careful.”

“Very careful.”

Elías continued reading. “And you have not grown weary.”

Marcos looked impressed. “That’s remarkable. This is an exemplary church for us to model after.”

“Yes, but there's more,” Elías said.

The others waited quietly. Because they knew the passage wasn’t finished. Elías read the next line slowly.

“But I have this against you…”

The words settled over the table. Adrián frowned slightly. “Well sir. That changed quickly.”

Matías leaned forward. “What was the problem they faced?”

Elías read again. “You left your first love.”

No one spoke. Adrián finally said, “Ok. I didn't see that coming.”

Matías said, “With everything else they were doing right.”

“Yes.”

Marcos leaned back in his chair. “So the church was doctrinally careful.”

“Yes.”

“Morally serious.”

“Yes.”

“Disciplined.”

“Yes.”

“And yet Christ still rebukes them.”

“Yes.”

Adrián rubbed his chin. “So let me understand something.”

“What?” Marcos asked.

“Correct doctrine didn’t prevent the problem.”

“No,” Elías said.

Matías spoke. “Doctrine is essential.”

“Of course,” Elías replied.

“No one is questioning that.”

Marcos said, “But this passage shows something important.”

“What?”

“Correct doctrine alone does not guarantee spiritual life.”

Adrián leaned back. “I'm sensing a little discomfort.”

“Why?” Matías asked.

“Because a lot of churches measure health by how correct their teaching is.”

“Yes,” Marcos said.

“But Ephesus had correct teaching.”

Matías looked at the passage again. “They even tested false apostles.”

“Yes,” Elías said.

“They were careful.”

“And yet something had still gone wrong.”

Adrián looked thoughtful. “What exactly does it mean that they left their first love?”

Elías answered slowly. “It means their devotion to Christ had grown cold.”

“But their doctrine hadn’t, that's more important right?” Marcos asked.

“I think so.”

Matías sat quietly. Finally he said, “That’s subtle.”

“Yes,” Elías said.

“Very subtle.”

Adrián leaned forward again. “So a church can…”

He paused. “…teach the truth.”

“Yes.”

“Defend the truth.”

“Yes.”

“Protect the truth.”

“Yes.”

“And still lose something essential.”

The others nodded. Marcos spoke. “That might be the most dangerous situation we’ve seen yet.”

“Why?” Adrián asked.

“Because everything looks so right.”

Elías closed the Bible slowly. “Yes.”

“That’s exactly why Christ warned them.”

Matías spoke. “What did He tell them to do?”

Elías answered. “Remember where they had fallen from.”

“And return.”

The table sat quietly again. After a moment Adrián said, “So now we’ve seen three different kinds of churches.”

“What do you mean?” Marcos asked.

“A lively church like Corinth.”

“Yes.”

“A serious church like Galatia.”

“Yes.”

“And a correct church like Ephesus.”

Matías added quietly, “And each of them still had a problem.”

Adrián said, “Yes.”

“And that makes me wonder something.”

“What?” Elías asked.

Adrián looked around the table. “What would a church look like…”

He paused. “…that Christ actually approves?”

No one answered. Because the investigation had now revealed something none of them had expected. Carnality could appear in excitement. Carnality could appear in religious effort. And apparently… Carnality could even appear in doctrinal correctness. And if that was true…

Then recognizing a truly spiritual church might be far harder than they had ever imagined.

# Chapter 14

## The Successful Church

For a while the table was quiet. Not uncomfortable. Just thoughtful. They had now looked at three churches. Three very different kinds of churches.

And yet all three had received correction in Scripture. Adrián finally broke the silence.

“Well,” he said, “if we’re being fair…”

He looked across the table. “…we should probably look at the last one.”

Marcos said, “You mean Laodicea.”

“Yes.”

Matías leaned back slightly. “That one seems pretty straight forward.”

Adrián smiled. “That’s what everyone says.”

Marcos opened the Bible and found the passage. Revelation 3. He read aloud.

“To the angel of the church in Laodicea write…”

Adrián leaned forward. “Let’s hear it.”

Marcos continued. “I know your deeds…”

Matías said, “So they were active.”

“Yes.”

Marcos read the next line. “That you are neither cold nor hot…”

Adrián frowned slightly. “That’s the famous part.”

“Yes.”

Marcos continued. “So, because you are lukewarm, and neither hot nor cold, I will vomit you out of my mouth.”

Adrián sat back. “That’s strong.”

“Yes,” Elías said.

Matías spoke. “I’ve always heard that explained as spiritual apathy.”

“Yes,” Marcos said.

“That’s the usual explanation.”

Adrián rubbed his chin. “But the passage keeps going.”

Marcos nodded and continued reading. “Because you say, ‘I am rich, and have gotten riches, and have need of nothing;’”

Adrián laughed. “Well sir. That sounds confident.”

“Yes.”

Marcos finished the sentence. “And you do not know that you are wretched and miserable and poor and blind and naked.”

The words settled over the table. Adrián finally said, “That’s quite a difference.”

Matías said, “They were certain they were doing well.”

“Yes,” Elías said.

“But Christ said the opposite.”

Marcos closed the Bible slightly. “What interests me is what they believed about themselves.”

“What do you mean?” Adrián asked.

“They said they needed nothing.”

Matías thought about that. “That suggests success.”

“Yes.”

Adrián leaned forward. “Successful churches are usually admired.”

Marcos said, “Large attendance.”

“Many programs.”

“Active ministries.”

Matías added, “Financial stability.”

Elías spoke. “And influence.”

Adrián smiled. “That sounds like the kind of church everyone wants.”

“Yes,” Marcos said.

“But apparently Christ saw something different.”

Matías asked the obvious question. “What exactly was the problem?”

Marcos looked back at the passage. “They believed they were spiritually rich.”

“But they weren’t,” Adrián said.

“Exactly.”

Elías spoke. “Actually, that may be the most dangerous condition yet.”

Adrián tilted his head. “Why?”

“Because they were completely unaware.”

Matías said, “They didn’t feel needy.”

“They felt successful.”

Adrián thought about that. “So Corinth had visible problems.”

“Yes.”

“Galatia had doctrinal confusion.”

“Yes.”

“Ephesus had lost love.”

“Yes.”

“But Laodicea…”

He paused. “…didn’t even know anything was wrong.”

Marcos said, “Yes.”

Elías spoke carefully. “That may be why Christ spoke so directly to them.”

Adrián looked at the passage again. “What did He tell them to do?”

Marcos answered. “He told them to buy gold refined by fire.”

“White garments.”

“And eye salve.”

Matías said, “So they needed something they didn’t realize they lacked.”

“Yes.”

Adrián leaned back. “That’s unsettling.”

“Why?” Elías asked.

“Because it means a church can look strong…”

He paused. “…and still be spiritually empty.”

The table sat quietly again. Finally Matías spoke. “So now we’ve seen four.”

Adrián said, “Yes.”

“Corinth.”

“Galatia.”

“Ephesus.”

“Laodicea.”

Marcos spoke. “And all four had something in common.”

Adrián raised an eyebrow. “What?”

Marcos looked around the table. “Christ rebuked them.”

Elías said, “Yes.”

Matías looked thoughtful. “That means something important.”

“What?” Adrián asked.

“That carnality…”

He paused. “…does not always look the same.”

Adrián said, “No.”

“Sometimes it looks exciting.”

“Sometimes it looks serious.”

“Sometimes it looks correct.”

“And sometimes…”

He gestured slightly. “…it looks successful.”

The table went quiet again… the espresso machine could be heard steaming in the background... Because something had now become unavoidable. Carnality could wear many different masks. And if that was true… Recognizing it might be far harder than anyone expected. Adrián finally said what all of them were thinking.

“So let me ask the obvious question.”

The others looked at him.

“If all four of these churches were carnal in different ways…”

He paused. “…what exactly **is** carnality?”

No one answered immediately. Because that question was about to change the entire direction of their investigation.

# Chapter 15

## What Do We Mean by “Flesh”?

For a while no one spoke. Four churches. Four very different pictures. Corinth. Galatia.

Ephesus. Laodicea. Each one different. Yet each one corrected. Adrián finally broke the silence.

“So now what?”

Marcos looked at him. “What do you mean?”

“I mean we've been talking about these churches like we understand the problem.”

Matías said, “Carnality.”

“Yes,” Adrián said.

“That word keeps coming up.”

Elías folded his hands on the table. “It does.”

Adrián leaned forward. “But I’m starting to realize something.”

“What?” Marcos asked.

Adrián shrugged. “I’m not sure I actually know what it means.”

The others looked at him. Matías spoke first. “You mean *flesh*?”

“Yes.”

Adrián said, “We keep saying Corinth was fleshly.”

“And Galatia.”

“And possibly Ephesus.”

“And Laodicea.”

He paused. “But what exactly are we saying?”

Marcos leaned back. “That’s a fair question.”

Matías answered quickly. “It’s sin.”

Adrián looked at him. “Obvious sin?”

“Yes.”

“Immorality.”

“Drunkenness.”

“Those kinds of things.”

Adrián said, “That would explain Corinth.”

“Yes,” Matías said.

“But not the others.”

Marcos spoke. “Galatia wasn’t immoral.”

“No,” Matías admitted.

“They were serious about obedience.”

Elías added quietly, “Very serious.”

Adrián rubbed his chin. “And Ephesus was doctrinally careful.”

“Yes.”

“And Laodicea was confident and successful.”

The table grew quiet again. Finally Marcos said what all of them were thinking. “So carnality must mean something more than obvious sin.”

Matías frowned slightly. “What else could it mean?”

Elías spoke. “The word Paul uses in 1 Corinthians is interesting.”

Marcos looked up. “Which word?”

“*Sarkikos.*”

Adrián smiled faintly. “Well sir… Greek, then, huh? Here we go.”

Elías continued calmly. “Yes, it literally means *of the flesh*.”

Matías said, “Honestly, that doesn’t clarify much.”

“No,” Elías admitted.

“But the context might.”

Marcos opened the Bible. “Let’s look at it.”

He turned to **1 Corinthians 3**. Adrián read aloud from his new WEB Bible. “Brothers, I couldn’t speak to you as to spiritual, but as to fleshly, as to babies in Christ.”

He paused. “That’s the passage. The WEB uses the word *fleshly*.”

Marcos said, “Yes.”

Matías frowned. “They were immature.”

“Yes.”

Adrián read further. “For insofar as there is jealousy, strife, and factions among you, aren’t you fleshly, and don’t you walk in the ways of men?”

He stopped. “That seems straightforward.”

Marcos said, “Jealousy and strife.”

“That’s the evidence.”

Matías leaned forward. “So *fleshly* means sinful behavior.”

“Possibly,” Marcos said.

“But the passage doesn’t stop there.”

Adrián looked down again and continued reading. “For when one says, ‘I follow Paul,’ and another, ‘I follow Apollos,’ aren’t you fleshly?”

The table was quiet. Matías spoke. “That sounds like pride.”

“Yes.”

“Division.”

“Yes.”

Adrián leaned back. “So worldliness or the flesh shows up as jealousy…”

“…strife…”

“…division.”

Marcos said, “Yes.”

But Elías was still looking at the passage.

“There’s something else here.”

“What?” Adrián asked.

Elías pointed to the text. “They were still called *brothers*.”

Matías said, “Yes.”

“And Paul still called them *infants in Christ*.”

Marcos leaned forward. “So they were believers.”

“Yes.”

“But still fleshly.”

Adrián thought about that. “That’s uncomfortable.”

“Why?” Matías asked.

“Because we tend to separate those two things.”

“What do you mean?”

“We assume flesh equals unbeliever.”

Elías shook his head. “Paul didn’t say that.”

Matías looked thoughtful. “So believers can live according to the flesh.”

“Yes,” Marcos said.

Adrián tapped the table lightly. “Okay.”

“So flesh is more than just immoral behavior.”

“Yes.”

“And it’s possible even in a church full of believers.”

“Yes.”

“But that still leaves the question.”

“Question? What question?” Matías asked.

Adrián looked around the table. “What exactly is the flesh?”

No one answered immediately. Each of them realized the same thing: they had used the word many times, but none of them had ever stopped to define it carefully from Scripture. Marcos finally spoke. “If we’re going to continue this investigation…”

He looked at the others. “…we probably need to answer that question.”

Elías said, “Yes.”

Matías folded his arms. “So where do we start?”

Marcos answered simply. “With the passages.”

Adrián smiled. “That sounds like a long evening.”

Marcos said, “It might be.”

Adrián leaned back. “Well…”

He looked at the Bible again. “…I guess we finally have our real question.”

The others looked at him. Adrián said it plainly. “If carnality can look like Corinth…”

“…or Galatia…”

“…or Ephesus…”

“…or Laodicea…”

He paused. “…then what exactly does the Bible mean when it says *flesh*?”

And with that question, the investigation finally turned where it should have started all along. To the Scriptures themselves.

# Chapter 21

## In Need of Nothing

Marcos arrived at Deb's Place carrying a folder. Adrián noticed it immediately.

"Oh, no."

Marcos looked at him. "What?"

"More reports?"

"Good morning to you too."

Elías smiled as Marcos took his seat. "How are you feeling?"

"Excellent."

"Have you recovered from last week?"

"Mostly."

Adrián looked at the folder. "Apparently not."

Marcos laughed. "These are just a few things I wanted to show you."

"You showed us quite a few things Saturday night."

"These are different."

"Of course they are."

The waitress approached.

"Coffee, gentlemen?"

"Please," Marcos said.

She filled their cups and looked at the folder. "Are you planning another conference?"

Adrián grinned. "Don't encourage him."

Marcos smiled. "Not today."

"Good. I haven't recovered from the last group you sent over here."

Marcos looked surprised. "What group?"

"Some of your volunteers came in for lunch. Fourteen of them."

"Really?"

"All wanted separate bills."

Adrián burst out laughing. "Fourteen separate bills?"

"Fourteen," she repeated. "And one of them wanted to know whether we offered a group discount."

Marcos shook his head. "I apologize."

"Don't worry. They were lovely people."

She glanced at Adrián. "Some customers are more trouble than others."

Adrián placed a hand over his heart. "After all these years."

She smiled and headed toward the counter. Marcos opened his folder. "Anyway, I thought you might be interested in the final figures."

Adrián looked at Elías. "He's unstoppable."

Elías laughed. "Let him show us."

Marcos spread several pages across the table. "We've finished reconciling the accounts. The financial results are even better than we reported at the banquet."

"Really?" Elías asked.

"Yes. A few additional contributions came in afterward, and several sponsors have already committed to next year."

Adrián studied one of the pages. "That's impressive."

"And the registrations?"

"Still coming in. We've already had inquiries from professionals who want to participate next year."

Elías examined the report. "You've developed quite a network."

"We have. And that makes a tremendous difference. When people know they can depend on you, they're willing to become involved."

Adrián said, "That makes sense."

Marcos turned another page. "We're also considering expanding the program. More sessions, additional community partners, perhaps another venue."

"Another venue?" Adrián asked.

"Eventually."

"Marcos, you just finished this one."

"Which is exactly when you should start planning the next."

Adrián sighed. "Some people never learn."

Marcos smiled. "Some people never plan."

Elías laughed. "You're both quite consistent."

Marcos gathered the papers. "Seriously, though, I think we've established something important."

"What's that?" Elías asked.

"That a church can become a real asset to its community. Not simply a place people visit on Sunday. A place that contributes something useful."

"There's certainly value in that," Elías said.

Marcos said, "And we've demonstrated that it can be done on a large scale without sacrificing financial responsibility."

Adrián looked thoughtful. "Your church must have a considerable number of professionals."

"Quite a few. Accountants, lawyers, counsellors, teachers, business owners, health professionals. People with years of experience."

"And they're willing to give their time?"

"Very willing. That's one of the things I'm most pleased about."

Elías smiled. "It must be encouraging to see people using their abilities to help others."

"It is."

Marcos closed the folder. "And we've got excellent leadership. Our finances are strong, our facilities are in good condition—especially now that we've replaced half the air-conditioning system—and our community relationships have never been better."

Adrián laughed. "Well, there's the air conditioning again."

"Don't remind me."

"At least everyone stayed cool."

"Everyone except me."

They laughed. Then Elías looked at the folder. "Marcos, may I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"How do you evaluate the spiritual health of your church?"

Marcos considered the question. "Well, we look at several things."

"Such as?"

"Participation. Giving. Volunteer involvement. Attendance. Whether people are engaged in the life of the church."

Elías said, "And what else?"

"Leadership development. Community impact. Financial stewardship. We review all those things."

Adrián stirred his coffee. "Those are the same things you measured at the event."

"Some of them, yes."

"Do you measure anything else?"

Marcos looked at him. "Like what?"

Adrián hesitated. "I don't know exactly."

Marcos smiled. "That's the problem with questions like that. Everyone wants a healthy church, although people don't always agree on what that means."

"Fair enough," Elías said.

Marcos continued. "At least with these things, we can see whether we're making progress."

Elías rested his hands on the table. "Can you always?"

Marcos looked at him. "What do you mean?"

"Can measurable progress tell you everything you need to know about a church?"

"Of course not. I never said it could."

"Then how would you recognize something important that wasn't being measured?"

Marcos considered it. "Well, you'd have to know what you were looking for."

"Exactly."

Adrián glanced at Elías. "Have you got something in mind?"

Elías reached for his Bible. "Actually, yes."

Marcos smiled. "I thought that question was going somewhere."

"It reminded me of something we haven't examined yet."

"Another church?"

"Laodicea."

Adrián leaned forward. "Ah. Our next candidate."

Marcos laughed. "Candidate for what?"

Adrián looked at him. "At this point, I'm afraid to say."

Elías opened Revelation. "Let's read what Christ says about it."

He found the passage. "Revelation chapter three, beginning at verse fourteen."

Marcos opened his Bible as well. Adrián followed. Elías began reading.

“To the angel of the assembly in Laodicea write: ‘The Amen, the Faithful and True Witness, the Beginning of God’s creation, says these things:’”

He paused. "Notice how Christ introduces Himself."

Marcos looked at the verse. "'The Faithful and True Witness.'"

"Yes."

"Why is that significant?"

"Perhaps we'll see."

Elías continued. "'I know your works, that you are neither cold nor hot. I wish you were cold or hot.'"

Adrián frowned. "That's an unusual thing to say."

"It is."

"'So, because you are lukewarm, and neither hot nor cold, I will vomit you out of my mouth.'"

Adrián looked up. "That's rather strong."

"Very strong," Elías agreed.

Marcos studied the passage. "What's He referring to? Their commitment?"

"Possibly. Let's keep reading before deciding."

Elías continued. "'Because you say, ‘I am rich, and have gotten riches, and have need of nothing;’'"

Marcos looked up. "Interesting."

Elías stopped. "What is?"

"They're wealthy."

"That's what they say."

"Yes, although being wealthy isn't necessarily wrong."

"No," Elías replied. "The passage doesn't say wealth itself is wrong."

Adrián leaned closer. "Read the rest."

Elías continued. "'And do not know that you are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked.'"

Nobody spoke for a moment. Marcos looked back at the verse. "That's quite a difference."

"Between what?" Adrián asked.

"Between what they say about themselves and what Christ says about them."

Elías said, "Yes."

Marcos read the verse again. "'You say, I am rich, and have gotten riches, and have need of nothing;'"

He paused. "And then He says they don't know their actual condition."

Adrián leaned back. "So they thought they were doing well."

"Apparently," Elías said.

"And Christ disagreed."

"Rather dramatically."

Marcos looked thoughtful. "I wonder what made them think they were doing well."

Elías turned toward him. "That's a good question."

"Was it their wealth? Their activities? Their influence?"

"We aren't told all the reasons behind their assessment."

Marcos said, "Then we should be careful about assuming too much."

"Agreed."

Adrián smiled. "Listen to you two. We've barely started, and you're already arguing about what we don't know."

Elías laughed. "That's usually where good interpretation begins."

"With an argument?"

"With recognizing what the text actually says."

Marcos looked at the passage. "Well, it certainly says they considered themselves wealthy and in need of nothing."

"Yes."

"And it says Christ saw them very differently."

"Exactly."

Marcos sat quietly for a moment. Then he looked at Elías. "Here's what I don't understand."

"Go ahead."

"If they were wealthy, perhaps they really did have resources. And if they had resources, they could accomplish things that poorer churches couldn't."

"Certainly."

"So why would Christ describe them as poor?"

Elías smiled. "Perhaps because He's talking about a different kind of wealth."

Marcos considered that. "Spiritual wealth?"

"That seems likely, given the counsel that follows."

He pointed to verse eighteen. "'I counsel you to buy from me gold refined by fire, that you may become rich; and white garments, that you may clothe yourself, and that the shame of your nakedness may not be revealed; and eye salve to anoint your eyes, that you may see.'"

Adrián looked up. "Buy from me."

"Yes," Elías said.

"Christ is telling them they need something from Him."

Marcos said, "Something they apparently didn't realize they lacked."

"That's how I read it."

Adrián looked at the passage again. "Gold, clothing, eye salve. Three things."

"And each corresponds to something Christ says about their condition," Elías replied.

"Poor, naked, blind."

"Exactly."

Marcos leaned back. "Then their problem wasn't simply that they had money."

"No."

"Or that they were successful."

"Not necessarily."

Marcos tapped the page. "It was that they believed they needed nothing."

Elías looked at him. "That certainly appears to be central to the rebuke."

Marcos was silent. The waitress approached.

"Everything all right, gentlemen?"

Adrián looked up. "Yes, thank you."

"More coffee?"

"Please."

She filled their cups. Then she noticed the open Bibles.

"Back to studying?"

Elías smiled. "Yes."

"Well, don't let me interrupt."

She turned toward Marcos. "And congratulations on your big event. I saw something about it on the news."

Marcos brightened. "Thank you."

"It looked wonderful."

"We were very pleased with how it turned out."

"You should be. It's nice seeing a church do something for the community."

"That's what we're hoping to accomplish."

She smiled. "Well, keep it up."

As she walked away, Marcos watched her for a moment. Then he turned back to his Bible. Adrián looked at him.

"That must be encouraging."

"It is."

"People really appreciate what your church is doing."

Marcos said, "They do."

Elías took a sip of coffee. "And there's nothing wrong with that."

Marcos looked at him. "I know."

Elías returned his attention to Revelation. "Let's look at verse nineteen."

He read aloud. "'As many as I love, I reprove and chasten. Be zealous therefore, and repent.'"

Adrián looked surprised. "He loves them."

"Yes."

"Even after everything He just said?"

Elías said, "That's what the verse says."

Marcos looked at the passage. "Then this isn't simply a declaration that He's finished with them."

"No. He's calling them to respond."

Adrián smiled faintly. "That's encouraging."

Elías continued. "'Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, then I will come in to him, and will dine with him, and he with me.'"

Adrián stared at the verse. "Wait a minute."

Elías looked up. "What?"

"He's speaking to a church."

"Yes."

"And He's standing at the door?"

Elías glanced down at the passage. "That's the image."

Adrián looked at Marcos. "That's a strange picture."

Marcos frowned. "Why?"

"Well, if it's His church, why is He knocking?"

Elías considered the question. "We should be careful not to make the image say more than it does. The invitation is addressed to anyone who hears His voice and opens."

"Still," Adrián said, "it's striking."

"It is."

Marcos read the verse again. "'then I will come in to him, and will dine with him, and he with me.'"

He paused. "That's rather personal."

"Very," Elías said.

Marcos looked thoughtful. "The whole passage is personal, isn't it? Christ is speaking directly to them."

Adrián said, "And He knows something about them that they don't know about themselves."

Elías closed his Bible halfway. "That's what makes this letter so searching."

Marcos glanced at the folder beside his coffee. Then he looked back at Revelation. "I suppose every church needs to hear what Christ thinks of it."

Elías said, "Even when His assessment differs from its own."

Marcos picked up his coffee. "Well, I certainly hope our church isn't like Laodicea."

Adrián smiled. "After last week, I should hope not."

Marcos laughed. Then he glanced at the open folder again. There were pages of figures inside. Attendance. Revenue.

Professional participation. Community partnerships. Plans for the coming year. He had spent months gathering those numbers. And they were excellent numbers.

He looked back at the words in Revelation. *You say, I am rich, and have gotten riches, and have need of nothing;* For the first time that morning, Marcos left his folder closed.
