Stegall’s “disagreement” points with hyper-grace (1 → 10, in his order)

Below is the numbered sequence he uses (“Here’s a ___ area of disagreement…”), summarized tightly but following his progression:

  1. Over-elevating love as the controlling/primary attribute of God in a way that pushes down holiness/wrath/justice (he critiques the “God is love, therefore not wrath/justice” style argument). SermonAudio+2SermonAudio+2
  2. Rejecting/softening propitiation and penal substitution, preferring “expiation only,” because (he says) they don’t want God viewed as judge with wrath/justice satisfied at the cross. SermonAudio+1
  3. (Some) embracing universalism / post-mortem second chance / annihilation, instead of eternal conscious punishment (as he presents it). SermonAudio
  4. Claiming believers are always pleasing to God and God is never disappointed, including rhetoric that treating “aiming to please God” as legalism. SermonAudio+1
  5. Denying a distinction between judicial forgiveness and parental forgiveness for believers—specifically pushing back on the idea that ongoing confession (for family-fellowship) matters, and treating that as “law not grace.” SermonAudio+2SermonAudio+2
  6. Denying progressive/practical sanctification (or reframing it so heavily as “behavior” that it functionally dismisses real growth/transforming). SermonAudio+1
  7. Saying believers should never be called “sinners” (saints only), sometimes extending to denial of an ongoing sin nature in believers. SermonAudio+1
  8. Teaching that the Holy Spirit does not convict believers of sin (only unbelievers), sometimes on wordplay (“convict” ⇒ “convict/convicted criminal”). SermonAudio+1
  9. Treating it as legalistic to view oneself positionally as a servant (son only), and (in his Romans 6 discussion) downplaying the “present/yield yourselves” thrust that he thinks belongs with position truth. SermonAudio+2SermonAudio+2
  10. Being non-dispensational (as he frames it) and therefore often denying degrees of reward for believers and conflating the Judgment Seat of Christ with final judgment for unbelievers. SermonAudio+1

Broader context you asked about: who “accuses” Free Grace, and who critiques hyper-grace?

If you want names (not just “camps”), these are some of the most visible streams:

  • Free Grace critics (often via the Lordship Salvation controversy): John MacArthur and many Reformed-leaning voices argue the debate is about the nature/fruit of true faith (and frequently critique “easy believism”). Grace to You+1
  • Free Grace critics (academic/pop-evangelical): Wayne Grudem’s book/argument is a well-known “no” from a mainstream evangelical systematic-theology lane. Grace Life+1
  • Hyper-grace critics (charismatic/evangelical): Michael L. Brown is one of the best-known critics who wrote a dedicated book specifically warning against “hyper-grace.” Internet Archive+1
